The four changes

1 Banks remember rescues (Moral Hazard)

Today
A rescue costs money and angers two interest groups. When the panic ends, nothing remains of it.
Change
Each rescue adds one point, up to four. Each point makes banks lend more, put more of it into goods that are already hot, and makes the next rescue dearer.
Your levers
Let a panic run its course: one point goes at once. Wait: one point fades every few years. Pass Company Law: points fade twice as fast.
The choice
Rescue every time and you get fast growth on borrowed money, with each rescue costing more than the last. Refuse and you take a longer, harder panic now for steadier banks later.

2 Credit Policy goes through politics

Today
Needs Central Banking. Then it is an instant three-way switch. Industrialists in government like Loose, Intelligentsia like Tight (2 approval each way).
Change
Available from the first day at half strength. Before Central Banking, Tight or Loose needs an interest group in government that wants it, the same rule the base game uses for proposing laws. With Central Banking the bank can act alone at full strength, and groups in government that dislike the stance lose approval.
Wider
Two more groups take a side, so most governments lean one way: Rural Folk want Loose (they are in debt), Landowners want Tight (they live on rents and bonds).
Your levers
Who you bring into government. When you research Central Banking.
The rhythm
When a government changes and a stance loses its backer, you are told, and it returns to Normal after six months unless you restore the backing.

3 One line for the market's temperament

Today
Your economic law, Financial Districts, Manor Houses and finance technologies already set how big booms grow and how hard they end. The figures are spread over several tooltips, under the name Market Conditions.
Change
One word on the card: Cautious, Steady, Lively or Wild. Its tooltip lists each cause with the base-game action that moves it. No rule changes: this only shows what is already there.
Your levers
The Economic System law. Who owns your buildings (Financial Districts against Manor Houses). Finance technologies.
Replaces
Market Conditions.

4 Booms show the patient route

Today
A boom that cools slowly already ends softer, by about a third for each year it takes. Nothing in the game tells you so. The row offers only Restrain and Encourage.
Change
The row's tooltip says what the ending would cost now, that slow cooling shrinks it, and which buildings add supply. No rule changes.
Your levers
Build supply early and let the price settle: free, but slow. Or Restrain: fast, and it costs authority every month.

What stays the same

  • The confidence gauge, Investor Interest levels, booms, panics, the Long Depression, Banking Reforms and Financial Centres.
  • No new tab, no new window, no new buttons. Each piece sits inside a section the tab already has.
  • Named concepts go from 25 to 23: Moral Hazard and Temperament come in; Market Conditions goes out; Correction, Bust and Collapse share the Boom End tooltip (the three words stay on screen).
  • Foreign lending and hot money are not touched.

Two corrections to what I told you before

  • I said political cost was new. Credit Policy already moves the approval of two interest groups. Change 2 widens that and adds the backing rule.
  • I said the patient route had to be added. The rule already exists; it is only invisible. Change 4 shows it.

So changes 3 and 4 only display what exists and cannot upset the balance. Changes 1 and 2 alter the rules and need test games.

Numbers that test games must settle

All of these are first guesses.

  • Moral Hazard: +10% lending and +25% rescue cost per point; one point fades every 5 years.
  • Credit Policy before Central Banking: half the strength of today's Tight and Loose.
  • Where the lines fall between Cautious, Steady, Lively and Wild.
  • Whether panics stay near today's 7 to 8 per 100 country-years, and whether computer-run countries still behave sensibly.
  • The target: about one decision worth making per game year, counted in test games.
