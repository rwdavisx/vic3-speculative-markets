The credit ladder

The limit is now 3 months, plus 3 with each of Central Banking, Investment Banks and International Exchange Standards. The bonus and the danger are now both bigger, and both run to 12 months: higher risk, higher reward. Up to 10 months a crash costs what it did before; the new danger sits on top of that. From about 9 months a crash costs more than the boom pays. The chart shows average throughput bonus less Bad Debts if a country rides to each limit and never tightens. It leaves out what the extra money builds and the investment lost in a crash.

A stress test on the 4 real games: every country gets the limit and the techs from the start and never tightens. It can't see GDP. Even riding, leverage averages only about 2 months, because lending needs confidence above 60 and each panic halves the debt. Lending at 40% means debt can reach 12 if income stays flat, and about 10 if income grows 1% a month. Of 37,490 logged country-months, 172 had Investment Banks and none had Exchange Standards, so the upper steps need the full-length real game.

What sets off a panic

The biggest single push on confidence in the three months before each panic. I suspected panics spreading between countries were the missing piece. They're the smallest slice, so that rebuild isn't needed.

The largest push isn't the only cause, as Codex points out: a small foreign hit can still tip a country already near the line. Removing the scaling of foreign hits changed the exam by only 0.02.

What each dial moves

What Codex thinks

Its verdict, third review

It ran the checks itself and confirmed the earlier fixes. It advised against my three small credit changes, so I dropped them. It then reviewed the bigger bonus and danger: no script errors, but it warns that nothing rules out a long investment collapse after the worst crash, because the model holds the economy fixed. The model says nothing about late game: that's blindness, not safety. Its first verdict still stands: useful for provisional sizing, with clean validation unproven.

What it caught across its reviews, now fixed or withdrawn
  • A panic length of 210 days added a month to the shortest laws too. Now 195, which changes no country.
  • Scaling predictions up by 1 / 0.65 wasn't valid. Removed.
  • Candidates were corrected against the wrong set of games. Now run on the 4 real games alone.
  • Two parts of the replay still read today's drift. Fixed, and a fresh read of every log gave identical results.
  • The cooling comparison isn't clean. Reworded.
  • Tight can't be waved away. Claim withdrawn.
  • "Worse off" past 6 months claimed too much: the measure is only part of the trade-off. Reworded.
  • The credit tooltip showed half the real bonus, and two gaps in the new AI rule. Fixed in the mod; the running game has the old tooltip, which changes nothing but the display.
  • The test of 40% lending at the top never ran. Rerun with the techs active: Severe crashes more than doubled.

Still standing from its first review: only 6 games sit behind the 30 predictions, 18 of 30 panic predictions fall in a range that should catch about 27, and dropping full contagion is only provisional. A separate review of the game scripts found nothing game-breaking: 0.7 saves carry over and the AI can't get stuck. It did find that the game never showed the limit, which is now fixed. None of this has run in the game.

What's left

Offline, still open
  • The model reads only 38 of 190 settings one by one. Wars, slumps and foreign panics are now measured as whole groups.
  • Tight months still fail the exam. Tight feeds lending and debt, so this matters: a miss that size could move panics by about 0.3 and Mania by about 0.8. That stays inside history's range, but it remains unsized.
  • Only 4 real 0.8 games sit behind the baseline, each a 20-year slice from a save.
  • The credit ladder is in at 3, 6, 9, 12. Logs stop near 1885, so its upper steps need the full-length real game.
  • The bigger bonus can't be sized here: the model reads panics, and the bonus doesn't feed them, so it can't see what +24% does to an economy.
