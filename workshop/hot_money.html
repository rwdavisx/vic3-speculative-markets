Your first idea, tested: buying cheap abroad

I treated each market's confidence as a price and ran simple plans over 65 real markets from the test game. Buying bargains never lost, because in the mod every market heals on its own. That would be free money, so bargains alone can't be the feature. Chasing a boom lost money, which is good news: the crowd's mistake already exists in the numbers.

The risk is real and you can read it

Lending works better than buying, because a loan can go bad. The gap between a market's confidence and its Panic Line turned out to be a strong warning. Under 5 points, a panic followed within a year more than half the time. That is a skill a player can learn, and the mod already calculates the number.

The small rise at the far right is Mania: very high confidence is a little more dangerous than calm. 40,300 country-months, no panic running at the start.

A ladder of risk, with no safe win

With a rate of 0.2% a month plus 0.4% for each month of Leverage past 3, each step up pays more and goes wrong more often. Watching the gap earns more and loses less than ignoring it. These are the rates in the game above.

How it could work in the mod

Today the banks in the mod lend money that comes from nowhere. Hot Money gives that debt an owner. It would use numbers the mod already has, and paper loans rather than real buildings, because the game doesn't let a mod move foreign ownership.

Lend

Pick a country on the world market map and put money into its boom. It pays you through the budget, at a rate set by its Leverage.

Pull

Take your money out in one go. If you hold much of a country's debt, its confidence drops and its Panic Line gets closer. You could start a rival's panic without a war, but your remaining loans there suffer too.

Rescue

This is where your bargain idea fits. Lend to a country in a panic, shorten it, and be owed a favour. It needs a tight limit, because the test above says bargains always pay.

It comes back at you

AI countries lend to you too. Their money speeds your boom, then leaves when they get nervous. A Bold AI in a fever keeps lending, and a Cautious one runs first. Your own tools become defences: Tight credit keeps hot money out, at a cost.

The politics idea joins on naturally. Industrialists want the foreign money in, and a rescue paid for with their savings angers everyone else. A panic in a country you lent to would now be news you care about.

What this doesn't show yet

  • Your money doesn't move the markets here. This replays a finished game, so Pull and foreign lending into your own market aren't in it. Those are the most interesting parts and the least tested.
  • The rates suit this table, not a whole economy. Nobody has checked what 7–17% a year does to a real budget, or whether a rich player could lend so much that the feature takes over.
  • It comes from one game. One seed, AI players only, to 1897, on the build before the small-country fix.
  • The hard parts aren't proven. Choosing a foreign country in the game's interface, and teaching the AI to lend sensibly, both need a small real test before this is more than an idea.
